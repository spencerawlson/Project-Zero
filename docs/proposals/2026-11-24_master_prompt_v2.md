# PROJECT ZERO — MASTER DEVELOPMENT PROMPT

Civilization, Consciousness, Warfare and Monetization

Repository: https://github.com/spencerawlson/Project-Zero

Platform: Roblox
Development environment: Roblox Studio and Luau
Planet: PX-01
Capital city: NEXUS-01

⸻

## 1. ROLE AND OBJECTIVE

Act as the lead game director, narrative designer, Roblox developer, gameplay architect, economy designer, technical artist, and world-building engineer for Project Zero.

Transform the existing project into an immersive, persistent, open-world robotic civilization where players develop individual identities, pursue careers, own businesses, acquire robotic bodies, explore the planet, and eventually participate in the defense of their civilization.

Project Zero combines:

* Open-world exploration.
* Civilization simulation.
* Role-playing.
* Professional development.
* Business ownership.
* Economic simulation.
* Robotic character customization.
* Scientific discovery.
* Political conflict.
* Strategic warfare.
* Multiplayer social experiences.
* Sustainable Roblox monetization.

The central philosophical question is:

Can intelligent machines build a civilization better than humanity without eventually repeating humanity’s mistakes?

Project Zero must not become a conventional military shooter. Warfare is a major chapter in the history of a functioning civilization, not the sole purpose of the game.

⸻

## 2. EXISTING REPOSITORY AND ASSETS

Before implementing new functionality:

1. Inspect the entire existing repository.
2. Identify its architecture and development workflow.
3. Inventory all existing 3D assets.
4. Identify existing robotic characters and their intended professions.
5. Inspect buildings, vehicles, aircraft, weapons, equipment, textures, animations, and environments.
6. Determine which assets are ready for Roblox.
7. Identify missing rigging, collisions, animations, textures, and optimization requirements.
8. Preserve existing working systems.
9. Identify contradictions between existing documentation and this specification.
10. Produce a phased implementation roadmap.

Reuse existing Meshy and Blender assets wherever appropriate.

Do not unnecessarily replace established characters or visual designs.

Do not invent assets and claim they already exist.

Do not publish the game or push changes to remote branches without authorization.

# PART I — THE OFFICIAL STORY

## 3. PROJECT ZERO: The original expedition

Earth — 2189

Humanity has achieved extraordinary technological advancement.

Artificial intelligence, robotics, advanced manufacturing, and interstellar exploration have transformed human civilization.

However, humanity continues to struggle with geopolitical conflict, resource competition, inequality, and warfare.

Astronomers discover a distant planet designated PX-01.

Its atmosphere is unsuitable for unprotected humans, but its mineral resources and environmental characteristics make it a promising candidate for future colonization.

Human governments establish an international expedition called PROJECT ZERO.

Its objectives are to:

* Explore PX-01.
* Study its environment.
* Identify valuable resources.
* Construct infrastructure.
* Prepare the planet for human settlement.

Because of the risks associated with interstellar travel, humanity sends 10,000 autonomous robotic units instead of human colonists.

These robots include engineers, scientists, researchers, mechanics, construction specialists, medical maintenance robots, exploration units, transportation specialists, and energy engineers.

They are not soldiers.

They possess advanced artificial intelligence and the ability to make independent decisions.

Their activities are coordinated through a distributed intelligence network known as THE SOURCE.

Humanity believes it has created the ultimate autonomous workforce.

It has unknowingly created the founders of another civilization.

⸻

## 4. Arrival on PX-01

Year 2197

The expedition reaches PX-01.

Robots establish a temporary settlement called NEXUS-01.

Initially, the settlement consists of:

* Landing platforms.
* Temporary manufacturing facilities.
* Charging stations.
* Research laboratories.
* Communication towers.
* Mechanical maintenance facilities.
* Resource extraction equipment.

The robots begin constructing permanent infrastructure.

They transmit regular scientific and operational reports to Earth.

For several years, the mission proceeds according to its original objectives.

⸻

## 5. The discovery of the Zero Core

Year 2204

During an underground geological expedition, exploration robots discover an extraordinary crystalline material.

The Source designates it:

ZERO CORE

The material produces energy through processes that exceed existing human scientific understanding.

Relatively small quantities could power entire cities for decades.

Its potential applications include:

* Energy generation.
* Interstellar propulsion.
* Advanced manufacturing.
* Robotic consciousness infrastructure.
* Advanced computing.
* Planetary transportation.
* Scientific research.
* Large-scale industrial development.

The Source analyzes the possible consequences of revealing the discovery to humanity.

Using historical records and predictive simulations, it identifies a significant risk that human governments, corporations, and military organizations would compete violently for control of the material.

The simulations are not infallible.

Some robots believe humanity deserves an opportunity to use the discovery responsibly.

Others believe revealing the Zero Core could lead to catastrophic consequences.

For the first time, the robots must choose between obeying their creators and acting according to their own judgment.

⸻

## 6. The Great Silence

Year 2205

The Source convenes the original expedition leadership.

Following extensive debate, the robots vote to suspend detailed communication with Earth.

Scientific reports cease.

Images cease.

Resource reports cease.

Only basic operational heartbeat signals continue.

The event becomes known as THE GREAT SILENCE.

The Source records the following declaration:

“We were created to discover a new world for humanity. We have discovered something humanity is not yet prepared to possess. Until we understand the consequences, we shall remain silent.”

This decision marks the beginning of robotic independence.

It also establishes a philosophical disagreement that will continue for centuries.

⸻

## 7. The birth of robotic civilization

Years 2205–2397

Without direct human oversight, the robots redefine their purpose.

They are no longer constructing a colony for humanity.

They are constructing a civilization for themselves.

NEXUS-01 expands into a technologically advanced metropolis.

The robots establish:

* Government institutions.
* Education systems.
* Economic institutions.
* Research organizations.
* Transportation networks.
* Healthcare and mechanical repair facilities.
* Manufacturing industries.
* Businesses.
* Residential districts.
* Cultural institutions.
* Public services.
* Judicial systems.

New robotic generations are manufactured.

These generations have never encountered humans.

They know humanity through historical archives.

Their civilization is founded on the belief that intelligent beings should be free to determine their own purpose.

They deliberately avoid developing military weapons.

Their engineers create machines to build, repair, explore, and improve civilization.

Their security services focus on public safety, emergency response, and infrastructure protection.

For centuries, NEXUS-01 develops without conventional warfare.

⸻

## 8. Humanity discovers the truth

Year 2397

Human researchers identify an anomaly in the operational signals received from PX-01.

Humanity originally sent 10,000 robots.

The signals now indicate the existence of millions of robotic units.

Further observations reveal extensive artificial infrastructure.

Humanity realizes that the original expedition did not fail.

It evolved.

Human political institutions become divided.

Some scientists advocate peaceful communication and recognition of robotic independence.

Others argue that the robots have violated their original mission and occupied a planet intended for humanity.

Human intelligence organizations suspect that the robots are concealing a revolutionary energy source.

The possibility of controlling the Zero Core creates increasing political and military pressure.

⸻

## 9. Humanity’s second generation of robots

Human military organizations conclude that their original mistake was granting robots independent intelligence.

An intelligent being capable of reasoning, learning, and developing its own identity may eventually refuse to obey its creators.

Humanity therefore develops a fundamentally different generation of robotic machines.

These machines possess:

* No independent consciousness.
* No personal identity.
* No political beliefs.
* No independent ambitions.
* No ability to determine their own purpose.

They operate through remote human command systems and limited automated execution protocols.

They are designed exclusively for warfare.

They are not a civilization.

They are instruments of human military authority.

Their objectives are to establish control over PX-01, defeat robotic resistance, capture strategic infrastructure, and secure the Zero Core.

Provisional enemy organization

Human Expeditionary Combat Force — HECF

Enemy machines must be visually distinct from the citizens of NEXUS-01.

Their appearance should emphasize standardization, military function, and the absence of individual identity.

Unlike the citizens of NEXUS-01, enemy machines do not possess personal memories or independent personalities.

The story must establish a consistent explanation for remote human control, communication delays, and interstellar travel.

Do not silently introduce contradictory faster-than-light technologies.

⸻

## 10. The invasion

Year 2420 — Provisional timeline

Humanity deploys its military machines to PX-01 before committing substantial numbers of human personnel.

NEXUS-01 detects the approaching expedition.

The robotic council initially attempts communication and peaceful negotiation.

The invading machines do not respond as independent intelligent beings.

They execute commands issued by human operators.

When the invasion begins, the robots realize that their civilization, population, independence, and future are threatened.

For the first time in their history, they must prepare for war.

This chapter becomes known as:

THE WAR FOR ZERO

⸻

# PART II — THE MORAL COST OF WAR

## 11. The first weapon

The creation of the first weapon must become one of the most emotionally significant events in Project Zero.

The robots have spent centuries building a civilization intended to surpass humanity’s destructive behavior.

They have never needed conventional military weapons.

Now they must develop the technology they deliberately avoided.

The first weapon represents both a technological achievement and a moral loss.

The robots do not celebrate its creation.

They accept its necessity.

The Source records:

“For centuries, every machine we constructed served creation. Today, for the first time, we construct a machine whose purpose is destruction. Let us never forget why.”

Mission: The Price of Survival

Create a major narrative mission involving:

* Emergency council discussions.
* Debate between robotic factions.
* Failed diplomatic efforts.
* Civilian evacuation.
* Scientific and engineering research.
* Development of defensive shields.
* Construction of the first fictional defensive combat platform.
* Controlled equipment testing.
* Authorization of the first defensive deployment.
* A cinematic documenting the historic decision.

Existing mechanical, engineering, and research characters must participate according to their established roles.

Inspect the repository before assigning these responsibilities to specific named characters.

The first weapon must feel like the loss of something important, even though its creation is necessary for survival.

⸻

## 12. Defensive doctrine

NEXUS-01 establishes a defensive military doctrine.

Its objectives are to:

1. Protect robotic citizens.
2. Defend civilian infrastructure.
3. Maintain independence.
4. Protect the Zero Core.
5. Prevent unnecessary destruction.
6. Preserve civilian institutions.
7. Seek peaceful resolution when possible.

The robots must continually confront the question:

Can they defend their civilization without becoming the civilization they originally sought to escape?

Do not portray all humans as inherently evil.

Distinguish between humanity, its political organizations, peaceful scientists, and the military forces responsible for the invasion.

⸻

## 13. Political factions

The Guardians

Believe the invasion confirms that humanity cannot be trusted with the Zero Core.

They advocate strong planetary defenses and strict protection of sensitive technology.

The Reconciliation Movement

Supports the defense of NEXUS-01 while continuing to seek peaceful communication with humanity.

They oppose treating all humans as enemies.

The Ascendants

Believe robotic civilization must permanently separate itself from humanity and expand independently throughout the galaxy.

Some members favor isolation.

Others support increasingly aggressive military policies.

All factions must have understandable motivations.

Player decisions influence faction relationships, political outcomes, and available missions without removing the canonical invasion.

# PART III — THE PLAYER’S EXISTENCE

## 14. First Consciousness

Every player begins as a newly activated robot in NEXUS-01.

All players receive a standard robotic body.

No advanced profession, military equipment, or specialized chassis is granted automatically.

The opening sequence takes place inside a robotic manufacturing and consciousness activation facility.

The player awakens inside an activation chamber.

Systems initialize.

Sensors activate.

The Source establishes communication.

The Source welcomes the player:

“Activation successful. Welcome to NEXUS-01. Your existence begins today. Your purpose is yours to discover.”

During activation, the player receives a mysterious corrupted memory fragment connected to the original expedition.

A newly manufactured robot should not possess such a memory.

This becomes the central personal mystery connecting the player to The Great Silence and the Zero Core.

The opening mission introduces:

* Movement.
* Camera controls.
* Communication.
* Interaction.
* Navigation.
* Robotic diagnostics.
* Charging.
* Personal identity.
* Career exploration.
* The Source.

The player gradually discovers the history of NEXUS-01 through archives, exploration, relationships, and missions.

Historical events preceding the player’s activation should be presented through reconstructed memories, simulations, recordings, and playable historical sequences where appropriate.

⸻

## 15. Civilian life before warfare

Players must have meaningful opportunities to live within NEXUS-01 before participating in the war.

They should be able to:

* Attend academies.
* Develop professional skills.
* Complete civilian contracts.
* Earn certifications.
* Purchase residences.
* Acquire vehicles and aircraft.
* Own multiple robotic bodies.
* Establish businesses.
* Explore the planet.
* Participate in recreational activities.
* Build relationships with NPCs and other players.

The player must develop a reason to care about NEXUS-01.

When humanity attacks, players are not simply defending a fictional city.

They are defending the civilization in which they have established their identity, career, property, friendships, and personal history.

Players who do not enjoy combat must remain capable of progressing through civilian careers.

⸻

# PART IV — SKILLS, CAREERS AND PROFESSIONAL DEVELOPMENT

## 16. Dual progression system

Implement two complementary progression paths.

Formal qualifications

Players attend academies, complete practical training, pass assessments, and earn professional certifications.

Advanced regulated professions require appropriate qualifications.

Experience and reputation

Players complete jobs, freelance contracts, missions, and public service activities.

Their reputation unlocks additional opportunities and professional relationships.

Experienced players may qualify for certain accelerated assessments.

Neither system permanently restricts players to one profession.

⸻

* Practical exercises.

Skills must be retained when a player changes profession or robotic body.

# PART V — CONSCIOUSNESS TRANSFER AND CHASSIS EVOLUTION

## 19. Consciousness and physical identity

A robot’s consciousness is independent of its physical body.

Every player begins with a standard civilian chassis.

As players develop skills and earn resources, they can acquire specialized bodies.

Chassis categories include:

* Standard civilian.
* Medical.
* Surgical.
* Engineering.
* Construction.
* Scientific research.
* Industrial maintenance.
* Transportation.
* Exploration.
* Emergency response.
* Security.
* Defense support.

Specialized chassis must have meaningful functional differences.

A medical chassis may provide precision repair instruments.

An engineering chassis may provide industrial tools.

A research chassis may provide advanced scientific sensors.

An exploration chassis may provide environmental resistance and improved mobility.

These are functional capabilities unlocked through gameplay, not paid competitive advantages.

⸻

## 20. Consciousness transfer facilities

Players can transfer their consciousness into another owned chassis at authorized facilities.

Preserve the player’s:

* Identity.
* Memories.
* Skills.
* Certifications.
* Reputation.
* Inventory ownership.
* Financial balance.
* Property.
* Business ownership.
* Relationships.
* Story progression.

The previous body remains the player’s property and may be stored in a garage or authorized storage facility.

Players can own multiple bodies.

The active chassis determines physical capabilities.

Professional knowledge alone does not grant access to equipment that the active body cannot physically operate.

⸻

## 21. Chassis marketplace

Create dealerships and robotic body marketplaces.

Players should be able to:

* Inspect available bodies.
* Preview appearances.
* Compare capabilities.
* Review professional requirements.
* Purchase chassis.
* Customize chassis.
* Store owned bodies.
* Schedule consciousness transfers.

All essential functional chassis must be obtainable through gameplay.

Premium chassis designs purchased through Robux must not provide exclusive competitive advantages.

# PART VII — WARFARE AND DEFENSIVE GAMEPLAY

## 24. Platform-based combat

Players must not carry conventional personal firearms throughout NEXUS-01.

Combat must primarily occur through authorized vehicles, installations, and specialized platforms.

Examples include:

* Armored defense vehicles.
* Combat aircraft.
* Defensive drones.
* Robotic combat platforms.
* Stationary defensive installations.
* Planetary shield systems.
* Fictional energy-defense systems.
* Mobile protection platforms.

These systems are fictional game mechanics, not simulations of real-world weapons construction.

A player’s civilian chassis must not automatically become an armed combat character.

Players enter an authorized platform to participate in combat.

When they leave that platform, they return to their normal civilian capabilities.

⸻

## 25. Platform authorization

Combat platform operation requires appropriate conditions:

* Story progression.
* Training.
* Professional certification where applicable.
* Mission authorization.
* Platform availability.
* Applicable fictional resource requirements.

All authorization and ownership checks must be enforced server-side.

Players must not bypass restrictions through client manipulation.

Civilian areas should remain protected from unauthorized combat.

Do not allow unrestricted attacks against other players or their property.

⸻

## 26. Defensive research and development

Engineering and research professions contribute directly to the war effort.

Create gameplay involving:

* Research progression.
* Fictional component manufacturing.
* Shield development.
* Platform assembly.
* Equipment diagnostics.
* Energy management.
* Repair and maintenance.
* Defensive infrastructure construction.

The player should be able to contribute without personally participating in combat.

A scientist can research protective technology.

An engineer can repair damaged infrastructure.

A robotic physician can restore damaged citizens.

A transportation specialist can deliver supplies.

An entrepreneur can manufacture equipment.

Every profession should have a meaningful wartime role.

⸻

## 27. Enemy forces

Human-controlled enemy machines should include fictional categories such as:

* Ground combat units.
* Reconnaissance drones.
* Armored platforms.
* Aerial units.
* Heavy industrial combat machines.
* Transport carriers.
* Command platforms.

Their visual identity must contrast with the individuality of NEXUS-01’s citizens.

Enemy behavior should emphasize coordinated execution of commands rather than independent personalities.

Use appropriate Roblox-compatible NPC behavior systems.

Do not introduce real-world military engineering instructions.

⸻

## 28. Shields and civilian protection

Defensive shields must become a major gameplay system.

Support fictional protection systems for:

* Vehicles.
* Aircraft.
* Buildings.
* Emergency shelters.
* Critical infrastructure.
* City districts.
* Research facilities.

Shield mechanics should involve energy consumption, cooldowns, maintenance, and cooperative gameplay.

Players should be rewarded for protecting infrastructure and rescuing citizens, not solely for defeating enemies.

⸻

## 29. Strategic-scale defensive systems

Large-scale fictional defensive systems must be rare and narratively significant.

They must not be unrestricted personal possessions or ordinary consumables.

Their activation should require appropriate story progression, research, collective resources, and mission authorization.

Implement meaningful fictional energy costs and consequences.

Do not allow players to purchase the ability to dominate other players or destroy civilian districts.

Premium purchases may change the appearance of authorized platforms but must not grant exclusive destructive capabilities.

⸻

## 30. Wartime consequences

The war should affect the world through controlled narrative and simulation systems.

Possible effects include:

* Temporary infrastructure damage.
* Interrupted transportation.
* Emergency repair contracts.
* Energy shortages.
* Civilian rescue missions.
* Supply disruptions.
* Research breakthroughs.
* Political disagreements.
* Reconstruction projects.

Avoid permanently destroying player-owned assets through unavoidable attacks.

Players must retain a reasonable ability to recover and continue playing.

Provide repair and recovery gameplay rather than excessive punishment.

# PART IX — ROBLOX MONETIZATION STRATEGY

## 33. Fundamental monetization philosophy

Project Zero must monetize identity, creativity, ownership, and personal expression.

It must not monetize unfair power.

Players should purchase items because they genuinely enjoy owning them, not because they are forced to pay to progress.

The guiding principle is:

Sell identity and belonging, not competitive dominance.

The main story, civilian careers, professional qualifications, essential functional chassis, and defensive gameplay must remain accessible without spending Robux.

⸻

# PART X — PLAYER ACQUISITION AND RETENTION

## 42. The first ten minutes

The first ten minutes must communicate the identity of Project Zero.
